## Recovery strategy comparison

1. Existing outer wrapper: small added containment for a healthy shared engine; cannot repair startup, trading or dependency faults. No separately accepted deployment artifact yet.
2. Independently composed last-known-good engine plus mandatory sole barrier and locked runtime: potential true application recovery, but new artifact/assurance scope required; not created here and never equivalent to unprotected baseline.
3. Independently controlled ingress: potentially contains disclosure despite engine failure, but must cover every domain/path, encoding, rewrite, private ingress and direct provider endpoint. None verified or configured; an untested WAF idea is not a recovery plan.
4. Approved full-service denial/outage: may stop disclosure at the cost of trading availability and broker-management implications; not authorised or tested here. Must not silently suspend live operations or assume external positions are managed.

Default: NO-GO until the accepted failure scenarios have an available, independently assured response and a named operator. There is no promise of universal zero-downtime recovery.
